## Executive conclusion

Halozyme is a commercially proven drug-delivery royalty platform, not a conventional drug-discovery biotechnology company. Its principal technology, ENHANZE, enables large biologic medicines that would otherwise be given intravenously to be delivered subcutaneously; pharmaceutical partners own and commercialize the medicines, while Halozyme receives royalties, milestone and license payments, and revenue from supplying its rHuPH20 enzyme. Halozyme also sells XYOSTED and Hylenex and supplies injector devices. [S1][S4]

The investment case rests on unusually strong current economics. Total revenue increased from $660.1 million in 2022 to $829.3 million in 2023, $1.015 billion in 2024, and $1.397 billion in 2025; operating cash flow increased from $240.1 million to $388.6 million, $479.1 million, and $651.6 million over the same periods. First-half 2026 revenue increased 45% to $857.7 million, royalties increased 47% to $548.4 million, and operating cash flow increased 66% to $421.0 million. [S2][S3][S4]

The quality of the growth is favorable because high-margin royalties are expanding faster than consolidated revenue. The risk is that the royalty stream is concentrated in a small number of partner medicines—especially DARZALEX—and depends on patents, partner execution, drug pricing, regulatory approval, and continued commercial uptake that Halozyme does not fully control. Halozyme is also attempting to buy its next growth platform through the Elektrofi/Hypercon and Surf Bio acquisitions while carrying $2.18 billion of debt against $231 million of cash and securities at June 30, 2026. [S1][S4][S8]

At $110.76, the shares trade at approximately 12.6 times the midpoint of management's $8.65–$9.00 2026 non-GAAP diluted EPS guidance, 15.5 times trailing free cash flow, and 18.0 times enterprise value to trailing free cash flow. The stock has already risen 51% over the preceding 52 weeks, so the valuation is inexpensive on management's adjusted earnings but no longer an undiscovered bargain. [S5][S8]

My decision is to **initiate**, sized at 2% of a diversified portfolio and purchased in two tranches. The position should not exceed 4% until product-specific patent durability, the 2027 convertible-note settlement, and clinical validation of Hypercon are better established. This is an attractive expected-return opportunity with concentrated, identifiable risks—not a low-risk stock. [Analyst judgment based on S1–S8]

## 1. The business in plain English

### What Halozyme sells

Halozyme's main product is intellectual property and technical know-how that changes **how another company's medicine is delivered**. ENHANZE uses recombinant human hyaluronidase, or rHuPH20, to temporarily break down hyaluronan under the skin so that larger volumes of medicine can disperse and be absorbed. This can turn a lengthy intravenous infusion into a faster subcutaneous injection and may permit administration outside an infusion center. [S1][S2][S3]

Halozyme monetizes this technology through four related revenue streams: royalties on partner-product sales; sales of bulk rHuPH20 to partners; upfront, target-selection, development, regulatory, and sales milestone payments; and device-development or supply revenue. Halozyme reported one operating segment, but its revenue disclosures separate royalties, product sales, and collaboration revenue. [S1][S4]

Halozyme also directly sells two proprietary products: Hylenex, a branded hyaluronidase used to improve subcutaneous fluid and drug absorption, and XYOSTED, a once-weekly at-home subcutaneous testosterone auto-injector. It supplies injector products to partners including Teva. [S1][S4]

### Why customers and patients use it

The commercial value proposition is less treatment time, potentially simpler administration, more convenient care, and greater capacity for clinics. The technology has moved beyond theoretical validation: by June 2026, ten commercial partner products were generating royalties, including five Roche products, two Janssen products, and one product each from Takeda, argenx, and Bristol Myers Squibb. [S4]

### Principal royalty products

- DARZALEX FASPRO/DARZALEX SC, commercialized by Janssen for multiple myeloma and related indications, generated $482.7 million of Halozyme's 2025 royalties. [S1]
- VYVGART Hytrulo, commercialized by argenx for autoimmune diseases, generated $157.2 million of 2025 royalties and $118.9 million in first-half 2026 royalties. [S1][S4]
- Phesgo, commercialized by Roche for HER2-positive breast cancer, generated $105.6 million of 2025 royalties and $58.6 million in first-half 2026 royalties. [S1][S4]
- Newer contributors include Tecentriq SC, Ocrevus SC, Rybrevant SC, and Opdivo Qvantig; older Herceptin and MabThera formulations are experiencing modest price erosion. [S1][S4]

### The next-generation strategy

Halozyme acquired Elektrofi in November 2025 and renamed it Halozyme Hypercon. Hypercon uses microparticles to concentrate biologics and reduce injection volume. Halozyme also acquired Surf Bio in December 2025; Surf Bio's platform has produced concentrations up to 500 mg/mL in non-clinical studies, with the objective of enabling a single auto-injector dose. [S1][S4]

The 2026 10-Q lists Hypercon collaborations with Janssen, Lilly, argenx, Vertex, and Oruka. The same filing records new ENHANZE agreements with Incyte and an undisclosed nucleic-acid therapeutic company. These agreements establish pharmaceutical interest, but neither Hypercon nor Surf Bio had produced an approved commercial royalty product as of June 30, 2026. [S4]

### What changed in 2023

Revenue increased 26% from $660.1 million in 2022 to $829.3 million in 2023, and operating cash flow increased from $240.1 million to $388.6 million. The 2023 filing attributed royalty growth principally to continued uptake of DARZALEX SC and Phesgo and the launch of VYVGART Hytrulo, while the consolidated business also contained a full year of Antares results following the May 2022 acquisition. [S3]

Customer concentration remained high in 2023: the four disclosed large partners represented 44%, 19%, 10%, and less than 10% of revenue, respectively. The largest partner's share had declined from 46% in 2022, but the top two still represented 63% of revenue. [S3]

### What changed in 2024

Revenue increased 22% to $1.015 billion, net income increased 58% to $444.1 million, and operating cash flow increased 23% to $479.1 million. Management again attributed cash-flow growth primarily to increased revenue, partly offset by working-capital spending. [S2]

The largest partner represented 41% of 2024 revenue, down from 44% in 2023, while the second-largest represented 17%, down from 19%. This was gradual diversification, not the elimination of concentration. [S2]

Halozyme completed the original $750 million repurchase authorization in June 2024 and adopted another $750 million program in February 2024. Convertible-note principal remained $1.525 billion at both December 2023 and December 2024. [S2]

### What changed in 2025

Total revenue increased 38% to $1.397 billion. Royalties increased 52% to $867.8 million, product sales increased 24% to $376.4 million, and collaboration revenue increased 8% to $152.3 million. Management attributed royalty growth mainly to VYVGART Hytrulo, DARZALEX SC, and Phesgo, with smaller contributions from newer products and a partial offset from erosion in older Herceptin and MabThera products. [S1]

DARZALEX royalties increased 29% to $482.7 million, VYVGART Hytrulo increased 444% to $157.2 million, and Phesgo increased 51% to $105.6 million. Together these three products generated approximately 86% of 2025 royalties; DARZALEX alone generated approximately 56%. [S1; percentages calculated from disclosed product royalties]

Product sales increased because of higher partner demand for bulk rHuPH20 and continued XYOSTED penetration; device-partner sales declined 3%. Management warned that bulk enzyme and device sales would fluctuate with partner requirements. [S1]

GAAP net income fell 29% to $316.9 million despite the revenue increase. Operating expenses doubled to $927.6 million, including $284.9 million of acquired in-process research and development associated with Surf Bio and a $48.7 million full impairment of ATRS-1902 after management discontinued the program. SG&A increased 34% because of patent-litigation costs, acquisition diligence and transaction costs, and compensation. [S1]

Operating cash flow nevertheless increased 36% to $651.6 million. Halozyme spent approximately $1.0 billion to acquire Elektrofi and Surf Bio, issued $1.5 billion of new 2031 and 2032 convertible notes, and used part of the proceeds to induce conversion of older 2027 and 2028 notes. Year-end convertible-note principal was $2.180 billion, up from $1.525 billion a year earlier. [S1][S2]

### What changed in first-half 2026

Revenue increased 45% to $857.7 million, royalties increased 47% to $548.4 million, net income increased 34% to $380.0 million, and operating cash flow increased 66% to $421.0 million. Management attributed the royalty increase to adoption of products launched since 2020, particularly VYVGART Hytrulo and DARZALEX SC, plus newer launches. [S4]

VYVGART Hytrulo royalties increased 133%, DARZALEX increased 27%, Phesgo increased 16%, and all other royalties increased 78%. Product sales increased 63%, led by a 165% increase in bulk rHuPH20 sales; collaboration revenue declined 14% because milestones are uneven and event-driven. [S4]

First-half operating expenses increased 56%. Cost of sales increased with enzyme volume, intangible amortization and R&D increased following the Elektrofi and Surf Bio acquisitions, and SG&A increased because of patent litigation, acquisition work, and compensation. Interest expense increased 24% following the 2031 and 2032 note offerings. [S4]

Halozyme used $332.7 million for repurchases during the first half and authorized a new $1.0 billion program through December 2028. Shares outstanding declined to approximately 114.0 million at June 30, 2026 from 117.6 million a year earlier. [S4]

## 3. Management's explanation and outlook

Management's recurring explanation is straightforward: royalty revenue is growing because newer ENHANZE products are taking market share and adding indications across more geographies. The filings identify DARZALEX SC, VYVGART Hytrulo, and Phesgo as the largest expected growth drivers and Tecentriq SC, Ocrevus SC, Rybrevant SC, and Opdivo Qvantig as contributors to the developing “other” category. [S1][S4]

After the second quarter, management raised 2026 guidance to total revenue of $1.835–$1.910 billion, royalty revenue of $1.220–$1.245 billion, adjusted EBITDA of $1.225–$1.280 billion, and non-GAAP diluted EPS of $8.65–$9.00. The guidance implies approximately 31%–37% total-revenue growth and 41%–43% royalty growth from 2025; those percentages are calculations from management's ranges and the 2025 audited results. [S1][S5]

On the August 6 earnings call, CEO Helen Torley characterized ENHANZE as a repeatable, scalable, diversified, and durable “compounding platform engine.” She said Hypercon was developing as a second engine and projected approximately $1 billion of Hypercon royalty revenue by the mid-2030s from an anticipated five to seven product launches. That is management aspiration, not contracted revenue or analyst fact. [S6]

Management also highlighted $4.0 billion of partner-reported global DARZALEX sales in the quarter, strong demand for the VYVGART prefilled syringe, four new collaboration agreements signed during Q2, and FDA priority review for Rybrevant Faspro in head and neck cancer. These statements identify real commercial and pipeline momentum, but the sales forecasts remain dependent on partner execution and regulatory outcomes. [S6]

Recent company news supports the pace of business development. During 2026, Halozyme announced Hypercon collaborations with Vertex and Oruka, an ENHANZE collaboration with GSK, and its analysis that a draft Medicare drug-price rule would have zero to minimal royalty impact through at least 2035. The collaboration facts are also recorded in the 10-Q; the Medicare statement is Halozyme's interpretation of a proposed rule rather than a final regulatory determination. [S4][S7]

## 4. What is going well

### 4.1 Commercial validation is substantial

Ten marketed partner products were producing royalties by June 2026, and the royalty base includes oncology, neurology, and immunology products from large pharmaceutical companies. This distinguishes ENHANZE from an early-stage platform supported only by preclinical data. [S4]

### 4.2 Revenue quality is improving

Royalties represented 62% of revenue in 2025 and 64% in first-half 2026, versus approximately 55% in 2022. Because royalty revenue does not carry the same manufacturing and selling burden as proprietary-product revenue, this mix shift should support high incremental margins, all else equal. [Analyst calculation and inference based on S1–S4]

### 4.3 Growth is becoming less dependent on only DARZALEX

DARZALEX remains the largest contributor, but VYVGART Hytrulo royalties increased from $28.9 million in 2024 to $157.2 million in 2025 and $118.9 million in first-half 2026. The “other” royalty category increased 78% in first-half 2026, indicating that newer launches are beginning to matter. [S1][S4]

### 4.4 Cash conversion is strong

Operating cash flow rose from $240.1 million in 2022 to $651.6 million in 2025 and reached $421.0 million in first-half 2026. Trailing free cash flow was approximately $806 million at September 3 because the business requires relatively little property investment, although working-capital timing can make individual periods volatile. [S1][S3][S4][S8]

### 4.5 Repurchases have reduced the share count

The market-data record shows 113.5 million shares outstanding at September 3, down 4.2% year over year. Repurchases increase each remaining share's claim on future royalties if management pays less than intrinsic value and does not impair the balance sheet. [S8; second sentence is analyst inference]

### 4.6 Internal controls have not produced a red flag

The 2025 10-K reported effective disclosure controls and internal control over financial reporting, and the auditor issued an unqualified opinion. The Q2 2026 filing reported no material change to critical accounting policies and no material change to the annual risk factors except an updated healthcare-pricing risk. [S1][S4]

## 5. What is not going well, or could go wrong

### 5.1 Revenue is concentrated

The three largest disclosed partners represented 41%, 17%, and 16% of 2025 revenue. DARZALEX represented approximately 35% of total 2025 revenue and 56% of royalties. A product-specific clinical, competitive, reimbursement, manufacturing, or patent problem could therefore matter materially even though Halozyme has many signed collaborations. [S1; percentages calculated from disclosed revenue]

### 5.2 Halozyme does not control the underlying drugs

Partners control most clinical development, regulatory submissions, launch timing, pricing, reimbursement strategy, manufacturing, and promotion. A partner can delay a program, devote fewer resources, fail to win approval, or underperform commercially while Halozyme bears the royalty consequence. [S1][S2][S3]

### 5.3 Patent protection is finite

The 2025 filing identifies foundational U.S. and European ENHANZE patents extending into 2027–2029, while management relies on additional formulation, manufacturing, method-of-use, and product-specific patents for longer protection. Patent validity, enforceability, and product coverage can be challenged, and alternative delivery technologies can compete without copying ENHANZE. [S1]

### 5.4 Replacement technology is not yet commercially proven

Hypercon and Surf Bio have produced encouraging non-clinical concentration data and attracted partners, but no product using either platform was commercially approved in the filings reviewed. Management's approximately $1 billion mid-2030s Hypercon royalty goal requires multiple clinical, regulatory, manufacturing, and launch successes that have not yet occurred. [S4][S6]

### 5.5 Acquisition evidence is mixed

Halozyme spent approximately $1.0 billion on Elektrofi and Surf Bio in 2025 and recorded substantial goodwill and acquired intangibles. It also fully impaired the separate $48.7 million ATRS-1902 in-process R&D asset after discontinuing development. That impairment does not prove the new acquisitions will fail, but it demonstrates that management can overestimate acquired-development value. [S1; last sentence is analyst inference]

### 5.6 Leverage and capital returns compete

June 2026 cash and marketable securities were $231 million against approximately $2.18 billion of total debt. The 2027 convertible notes mature March 1, 2027; principal is to be settled in cash, while any conversion premium may be settled in cash or shares. Halozyme simultaneously spent $332.7 million on first-half repurchases and authorized another $1.0 billion program. [S1][S4][S8]

The low coupons make cash interest manageable—market data show interest coverage above 46 times—but the principal remains an enterprise-to-equity claim and potential source of refinancing or dilution. Large repurchases are therefore only attractive if royalty durability is high and the shares are materially undervalued. [S8; second clause is analyst inference]

### 5.7 GAAP and adjusted earnings diverge

The 2025 GAAP result included acquired IPR&D, impairment, acquisition, litigation, and financing effects, while management emphasizes adjusted EBITDA and non-GAAP EPS. Some adjustments are non-cash or non-recurring, but acquisitions, patent defense, and stock compensation are economically relevant in a platform company. Valuation should therefore be cross-checked against cash flow and enterprise value rather than based only on non-GAAP EPS. [S1][S5; final sentence is analyst judgment]

### 5.8 Drug pricing and reimbursement are live risks

The Q2 2026 10-Q specifically updated the risk that government and private payers could reduce prices or access. Since royalties are generally linked to partner sales, lower drug prices or restricted coverage can lower Halozyme revenue even when treatment volumes remain stable. [S4]

### 5.9 Litigation creates asymmetric uncertainty

Halozyme alleges that Merck's subcutaneous Keytruda infringes MDASE patents, while Merck has challenged certain patents through PTAB post-grant review. Halozyme states that the MDASE dispute is separate from ENHANZE intellectual property. A favorable result could add value; an adverse result could eliminate hoped-for MDASE economics and leave legal costs without an offsetting return. [S1; final sentence is analyst inference]

The moat is real but time-limited. Halozyme's best defense is not one perpetual patent; it is repeated approval of new co-formulations, product-specific protection, partner switching costs, formulation knowledge, and successful migration to a second platform. [Analyst synthesis based on S1–S6]

## 7. Management and capital allocation

CEO Helen Torley presents ENHANZE and Hypercon as successive compounding platforms. Her operating record includes substantial royalty growth, multiple approved products, more partner agreements, and material share-count reduction. [S1–S6][S8]

The counterweight is aggressive capital allocation. Management spent about $1.0 billion on two platform acquisitions, increased convertible principal, recorded an impairment on another acquired program, and continued large repurchases. This strategy can create considerable per-share value if Hypercon succeeds and repurchases occur below intrinsic value; it can also amplify an ENHANZE maturity problem if acquired platforms disappoint. [S1][S4; second sentence is analyst scenario analysis]

For investment-committee purposes, management should receive credit for executing the current royalty model but not yet receive full valuation credit for the mid-2030s Hypercon target. The first meaningful clinical data, partner target progression, and eventual regulatory submissions should determine when that optionality enters base-case value. [Analyst judgment based on S4][S6]

## 11. Strongest bear argument

The bear case is that investors mistake a concentrated, finite patent royalty stream for a perpetual compounder. DARZALEX and two other franchises dominate royalties; foundational ENHANZE patents approach expiry; partners control the products; newer platforms remain uncommercialized; and management has added leverage, acquired intangible assets, and repurchased stock aggressively. If royalties mature before Hypercon becomes commercial, enterprise value can contract at the same time that debt and dilution absorb a larger portion of value. [Analyst synthesis based on S1–S8]

That argument is credible. The offset is that current growth is verified in audited and interim results, the portfolio of approved products is broadening, operating cash generation is substantial, and the current earnings multiple does not require management's full Hypercon ambition to succeed. [S1–S5][S8; final valuation inference is analyst judgment]
